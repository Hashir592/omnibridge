Summary: Critical 0, High 0, Medium 1, Low 1

## Confirmed findings

## S5-20261121-01 — Medium (maps to Major) — Console sign-ins are not logged, and no SEC-12 login alert covers platform admins
IDs: SEC-12, PLAT-1 (supports NTF-2, AUD-2)  CWE/OWASP: CWE-778 Insufficient Logging, OWASP A09
Evidence:
- `AuthService.#finishSignIn` (`apps/web/src/auth/service.ts:628-655`) records `auth.login` only when the session enters a workspace (`if (workspaceId !== null) … #auditIn(…)`). A platform admin with no membership finishes MFA in the `signed_in` stage, so nothing is written.
- `enterPlatform` (`service.ts:934-940`) moves the session to `platform` and writes nothing: no `app.platform_audit_events` row, no `app.auth_events` row, no workspace audit row.
- So the console's own audit (`platform_audit_events`) has rows for changes (status, flags, secrets, redrive), but none for "who entered the console, when, from which address". Reads such as `GET /platform/app-secrets`, workspaces, usage and dead letters leave no trace either.
- The T-0503 rules cannot see platform admins. `security.unusual_login` (new network) reads `auth.login` rows in a workspace's `audit_events` (`packages/db/src/security.ts:36-66`). `security_login_failures` and `security_locks` (db-main 0032) join `app.memberships` for the workspace being scanned. A platform admin who belongs to no workspace has no failed sign-in alert, no lockout alert and no new-network alert. (One who is also a member of a workspace gets alerts in that workspace, to its admins. That is the wrong audience too.)
- 20261117 NV-2 asked for SEC-12 login alerts. T-0503 added them for workspace members only.
Impact: platform admins have the widest reach in the product: they write app secrets, archive workspaces (which deletes every PII record of the workspace), toggle modules and redrive any job. Someone who has a platform admin's password and TOTP (or a live console session), and is inside the VPN range, can use the console with no alert. Afterwards nobody can tell when they entered, from where, or what they read. The changes they make are audited, but the sign-in that led to them is not. SEC-12 asks for alerts on unusual logins and for logs that can be reviewed. The IP allowlist and MFA make this harder to exploit, but the gap is in detection, and that is what SEC-12 is about.
Required fix (small, in T-0503's and T-0504's own patterns):
1. `enterPlatform` writes `platform.console_entered` to `app.platform_audit_events` with the user, the time and `request.ip`, through `console_audit`, in the same request. A sign-in that ends in the `signed_in` stage (no workspace) writes `auth.login` to `app.auth_events` (`recordAuthEvent`, as `#auditAccount` does), so a sign-in that leads to the picker is recorded too.
2. A platform-level rule, run once per sweep rather than per workspace (a `security.platform-scan` job, or a step in the existing sweep), raises platform notices (`raisePlatform`, the T-0501 platform scope) for platform admins:
   - a console entry from a network not used for a console entry in the last 90 days (same IPv4 or IPv6 /64 rule, and no alert for the first ever entry): `security.platform_unusual_login`;
   - failed sign-ins of a platform admin at or above `SECURITY_LOGIN_FAILURES` in 15 minutes;
   - every lockout of a platform admin.
   Severity: S1's call. Suggestion: critical for a new-network console entry (it emails every platform admin), warning for the others. Params are IDs and counts only.
3. The 0032 member rules leave platform admins out (`AND NOT u.platform_admin`), so no workspace admin is told about a platform admin's sign-ins.
Tests: `[SEC-12][PLAT-1] entering the console writes platform.console_entered with the address`; `[SEC-12] a platform admin's console entry from a new network raises a platform notice; a known network and the first ever entry do not`; `[SEC-12] failed sign-ins at the threshold and a lockout of a platform admin raise platform notices; workspace admins get none`.
Resolution: fixed in T-0507 (branch `task/T-0507-console-signin-audit`, ADR-076).
- `enterPlatform` writes `platform.console_entered` (user, time, `request.ip`) through `app.console_audit` in the same request. It now also refuses an admin without confirmed MFA before the move. A sign-in that ends with no workspace (picker, terms or password change) writes `auth.login` to `app.auth_events` (0035 widens the action rule NOT VALID; 0036 validates it).
- Platform rule `scanPlatformSecurity` (`apps/worker/src/security.ts`) runs once per `security.scan-sweep`, using the 0035 worker-only definers. It raises:
  - critical `security.platform_unusual_login` for a console entry from a network (IPv4, IPv6 /64) unseen in 90 days, never on the first entry;
  - warning `security.platform_login_failures` at or above `SECURITY_LOGIN_FAILURES` in 15 minutes (resolves when the count drops);
  - warning `security.platform_account_locked` for each lockout.
  Entries and lockouts are read over 24 hours (S1, 21 Nov), with one notice each (NTF-5).
- The 0032 member rules leave platform admins out (`security_platform_admin_ids()`, through the 0031 platform-admin policy).
- Console idle limit: `IDLE_MS` is already 30 minutes for every stage after MFA (USR-6), including `platform`. The 12 hours is the absolute limit. Regression test only.
- Tests:
  - `[SEC-12][PLAT-1] entering the console writes platform.console_entered with the address` (`apps/web/src/auth.int.test.ts`)
  - `[SEC-12] a sign-in that ends at the picker records auth.login per person`
  - `[PLAT-1] a console session idle for 30 minutes is ended (ADR-076)`
  - `[SEC-12][PLAT-1] a platform admin's console entry from a new network raises a critical platform notice; a known network and the first ever entry do not` (`apps/worker/src/security.int.test.ts`)
  - `[SEC-12] a console entry 2 hours old, missed by earlier sweeps, still raises (24-hour window)`
  - `[SEC-12] failed sign-ins at the threshold and a lockout of a platform admin raise warning platform notices; workspace admins get none`

Re-check (S5, 21 Nov 2026): **FIXED**.
- `enterPlatform` (`service.ts:956-970`) checks the flag and MFA, advances, then writes `platform.console_entered` through `console_audit` with `request.ip`. If that write fails, the rotated token never reaches the browser, so no console session can exist without its record.
- `#finishSignIn` writes `auth.login` to `auth_events` whenever no workspace is entered.
- The 0035 definers are granted to `ob_worker` only (EXECUTE revoked from PUBLIC). The console-entry query has the same shape as the member new-network rule and skips the first entry.
- `scanPlatformSecurity` runs from the sweep. Severities: critical for a new-network entry, warning for failures and locks. Params are IDs and counts. Failure notices resolve through `platform_notice_open_records`.
- The member rules exclude `security_platform_admin_ids()`.
- Tests: `auth.int.test.ts:1472`; `security.int.test.ts:311`, `:331` (24-hour window) and `:343` (workspace admins get none).

## S5-20261121-02 — Low (maps to Minor) — A sign-in can enter a workspace at the same moment as a suspend and outlive it
IDs: PLAT-2 (supports TEN-1)  CWE/OWASP: CWE-367 TOCTOU
Evidence:
- Suspend and archive end sessions once: `console_workspace_status` (db-main 0033, lines 96-135) locks the workspace row `FOR UPDATE`, sets the status, then `UPDATE app.sessions … WHERE active_workspace = p_workspace`.
- Entering a workspace checks the status with a plain read: `session_advance` (0033, lines 590-600): `SELECT EXISTS (… w.status = 'active')`, with no lock.
- Nothing checks the status on later requests: `resolveSession` (`service.ts:200-207`) and the tenant runner do not read `workspaces.status`. Revoking the sessions is the only enforcement.
- Order under READ COMMITTED: (1) `session_advance` locks the session row and reads `active`; (2) the suspend commits; its sessions `UPDATE` checks the committed row (`active_workspace` is still NULL or another workspace), so it skips that row without waiting; (3) `session_advance` sets `active_workspace` to the suspended workspace and commits. The session stays live in a suspended workspace until it goes idle or expires (up to 12 hours). For an archive, it lasts until the closure job removes the memberships.
- Not run live: the timing window is a few milliseconds. The order follows from PostgreSQL's documented READ COMMITTED behaviour (a plain SELECT does not wait for row locks, and an UPDATE skips rows whose committed version does not match its WHERE clause).
Impact: one user who was signing in at that moment keeps working in a workspace the operator has just suspended (for example, for non-payment or abuse). It is narrow and unlikely, but PS-032 says "sessions die at once" without exceptions.
Required fix: in `session_advance`, read the status with `FOR SHARE` (`SELECT 1 FROM app.workspaces w WHERE w.id = p_workspace AND w.status = 'active' FOR SHARE`). It then waits for a suspend that is in progress and sees the new status. If the sign-in goes first, the suspend waits for it, and then its sessions `UPDATE` finds the new session and revokes it. One migration with the same function signature. Test: `[PLAT-2] a sign-in racing a suspend never leaves a live session in the suspended workspace` (two connections, the advance held open with `pg_sleep`, or a barrier).
Resolution: fixed in T-0507 (0035, same signature). `session_advance` takes the workspace row `FOR SHARE` before it locks the session row, the same order as a suspend (workspace, then its sessions), so the two never deadlock. The status is read after that lock. Test: `[PLAT-2] a sign-in racing a suspend never leaves a live session in the suspended workspace (S5-20261121-02)` (`packages/db/src/auth.int.test.ts`). It runs two connections in both orders: a suspend in progress makes the sign-in wait and then refuse it, and a sign-in in progress makes the suspend wait and then revoke it.
Re-check (S5, 21 Nov 2026): **FIXED**. `session_advance` (0035) locks the workspace row with `FOR SHARE`, under `app.workspace_id` so RLS lets it see the row, before the session row, which avoids the deadlock. The test runs the real function against the suspend's own statements in both orders. In each order it checks that the other transaction is blocked (a 300 ms race) and then checks the end state: a refused sign-in at the picker, or a revoked session. A lock that was silently not taken would fail the first order.

## Needs validation

1. **The production edge and TRUSTED_PROXIES (M22).** The PLAT-1 network check is only as good as `request.ip`. Before go-live, check: (a) the shared edge's global options set no `trusted_proxies` for Caddy, so Caddy keeps replacing the X-Forwarded-For that clients send (it does by default); (b) how IPv6 clients reach the edge. With Docker's userland proxy and no IPv6 on the bridge, every IPv6 client arrives from the bridge gateway. That is exactly the address the dev allowlist trusts (`172.20.0.1/32`, ADR-073 (3)). The production allowlist must be the VPN range only and must never contain a Docker gateway. Check this in the M22 deploy task with a request from outside the VPN over IPv4 and IPv6, which should get 403.

## Hardening notes

1. **TRUSTED_PROXIES is wider than the edge.** The compose default is `uniquelocal` (`compose.yaml:197`): every private address. Web is on `ob_edge`, `ob_app`, `ob_db` and `ob_vault`, so any OmniBridge container on those networks (worker, pii-vault, the database helpers) can call `web:8080` directly and claim any client address in X-Forwarded-For, including a VPN address. It would still need a platform admin's session and MFA, so this is one missing layer, not a hole. In M22, set TRUSTED_PROXIES to the edge's own address: ADR-073 (3) pins the `ob_edge` subnet anyway, so pin the edge's IP in it.
2. **(Done, T-0412.)** **The PLAT-3 walk accepts any status below 500.** `walkPlatform` (`route-walker.ts:185-212`) counts a 403 or 404 as a pass, so a console route that refused by mistake (a CSRF or gate change) would still pass while proving nothing. Three routes have positive controls. Have the walk assert each route's expected 2xx (or a declared 404/409), as the isolation walk's own-workspace run does.
3. **One platform data key for TOTP secrets and app secrets.** The worker now holds `platform_key_get` and `secrets_kek` (ADR-073 (2), accepted). The AAD keeps the two apart (`user_auth/totp_secret/<user>` and `app_secrets/value/<name>`), and the worker cannot read `user_auth`, so nothing is open today. If the worker ever gets an auth definer, give app secrets their own key version first.
4. **(Corrected: not an issue.)** I misread the idle limit. `IDLE_MS` is 30 minutes for every stage after MFA (USR-6), the `platform` stage included; 12 hours is only the absolute limit. T-0507 added the regression test `[PLAT-1] a console session idle for 30 minutes is ended (ADR-076)` (`auth.int.test.ts:1507`). Nothing to do.

## Checked and holding (for the record)

- **Gate (PLAT-1):** no session gives 401, and every other case gives 403 (`contract.ts:166-169`, `service.ts:227-248`). The `platform` stage can be reached only from `signed_in`, `workspace` or `platform`, which come after MFA (`session_advance`), and the token and CSRF are rotated on the move. `platform_admin` and `mfa_confirmed_at` are re-read on every request and again in every definer (`console_require`). That closes 20261117 hardening 2. The allowlist is checked on `enterPlatform` and on every request, against `request.ip`. An empty or unset list denies everyone; a malformed one stops the boot. CSRF and Origin are checked on every write. A spoofed X-Forwarded-For through the edge is replaced by Caddy (int test at `auth.int.test.ts:1418`).
- **PLAT-3:** every `/platform/*` path is `auth: 'platform'`. Response schemas pass the static field allowlist. Fastify's zod serializer strips fields that are not in the schema. `params` are checked at raise time to be UUIDs, numbers and booleans. Dead letters read only the envelope's workspace and key from job data. `inviteFirstAdmin` refuses a workspace that already has an admin, so an operator cannot make themselves a member of a live tenant. The walk covers every platform route and fails if any route is missing.
- **Suspend and archive:** both revoke the workspace's sessions and open invites in the status transaction. The picker and `session_advance` refuse a workspace that is not active (apart from -02). Archive is final (409 afterwards). It queues `workspace.close` (`closure:<id>`) in the same transaction. The closure queues the `workspace_closure` deletion, removes memberships, and deletes the terms of people left with no workspace. `workspace_close_members` refuses a workspace that is not archived.
- **App secrets (PLAT-4):** AES-256-GCM under the platform data key, wrapped by `secrets_kek`. The AAD binds the name (int test: moved ciphertext does not open). The table is reachable only through definers: put, list and promote are for `ob_auth` and check the admin; `app_secret_read` (ciphertext only) is for `ob_tenant_rw`, which means web, worker and ops. `platform_key_get` is for `ob_auth`, `ob_worker` and `ob_ops`. `secrets_kek` is mounted only in web, worker and the rewrap job. Nothing is queued on a write. The log line and the audit row hold the name and slot. Problem bodies carry zod messages, which never include the input. Request bodies are never logged. The SPA field is masked, uses `autocomplete=off` and is cleared after a save.
- **Security alerts (T-0503):** thresholds fire at or above the limit, and the rules resolve once the count drops below (int tests at `security.int.test.ts:161-271`). Every vault read-full logs one row per record, so the mass-view count cannot be dodged with batches. `/v1/signals` is for the worker token only, and its strict schema returns user IDs, counts and canary event IDs (no field values).
- **Email (NTF-3, MAIL-3):** notification emails carry the type's registry title, the severity, the company's trading name and a sign-in link. The digest carries counts and a console link. Neither carries buyer PII or secrets. HTML is escaped, links are checked against the app origin, and subjects go through nodemailer's header encoding.
